🎣 You fell for the phishing simulation

This was a training simulation. No credentials left your browser — they were stored only in sessionStorage and are deleted when you close this tab. But here's exactly what a real attacker would have captured:

🚩 What made the clone suspicious?

1. The URL. The real portal is at northfield.edu. The clone was at a different path/file. Always check the address bar.
2. Urgency cue. The clone said "Your session has expired" to rush you into entering credentials.
3. Fake success. The clone showed a dashboard to keep you calm — then revealed a "session issue" only after your password was captured.
4. No real HTTPS indicator. The real site shows a lock and the correct domain; the clone's badge was just text.
5. Fake "forgot password". The clone also imitated the reset flow to harvest more email addresses — a second collection point.
6. Weaker validation. The real site enforces password strength at signup; the clone only checks that the password box isn't empty, because the attacker wants whatever you type.
7. The URL was a lookalike. The real page is index.html; the clone is indx.html — one missing letter. On the real internet this is typosquatting: northfield.edu vs northfıeld.edu (dotless i), northfield-edu.com, or northfield.edu.login-secure.com. The page can be pixel-perfect; the address is the tell.

🛡️ How to protect yourself

  • Type the university URL directly, or use a bookmark.
  • Check the domain carefully — lookalikes use typos and extra subdomains.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).
  • Use a password manager — it won't autofill on a fake domain.
  • Never enter credentials after clicking a link in an unsolicited email or SMS.
  • Treat "session expired — sign in again" prompts with suspicion: navigate to the site directly instead.
